A walk through space, and its shell sqrt2, base 10

Each digit is one step of length 1 in one of 10 directions spread evenly over a sphere, like a fly that changes course at every digit. 100,000 steps make a tangled path (colour = time: dark at the start, bright at the end). The shell is the tightest convex skin around the whole path, like shrink-wrap: its volume and surface area measure how much space the walk explores. Left: sqrt2; right: random digits. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.

Is sqrt2's shell unusual?

One random walk is not enough to compare against: the volume of a random walk's shell varies a lot from walk to walk. So the judge built the same shell for 500 random digit sequences. Volume is divided by steps1.5, because a random walk's shell grows like that. Below: the shell volumes of 200 random walks, and where sqrt2's falls: 76 of the 200 are smaller (38th percentile). Anything between roughly the 3rd and 97th percentile is ordinary. Verdict over all measures: indistinguishable from random controls (q = 0.862).
